# Use bans for deliberate match-throwing in v1

The v1 sanction for deliberately throwing a match is a ban. A Ballerz admin issues it directly and logs the reason. Evidence must support deliberate conduct, since an unusual team sheet or a poor result alone is insufficient.

The ban follows the Team NFT through a sale and bars the team from the rest of the current edition and the next edition of that cup. Completed results and awarded titles stand. A banned team still in contention forfeits its next tie under the existing knockout rules.

Apply the same ban to the responsible wallet. It is ineligible to enter the cup, acquire a team with a live entry, or take over as manager for that cup. If a delegated manager acted alone, apply the wallet ban to the manager. Include the owner if the evidence establishes their involvement.

A Ballerz admin may extend the remaining ban to another wallet when clear evidence shows it is being used to evade the ban. The admin logs the reason.

Only an eligible team owner or appointed manager can submit a team sheet. Ballerz checks eligibility at submission and records the wallet that acted. When clear evidence establishes proxy control, extend the remaining ban to the proxy's wallet and Team NFT. A team that is still in contention forfeits its next tie. Ordinary tactical advice remains allowed when the registered manager makes the decisions.

Evidence of proxy control must establish that the banned person made a match decision and the registered manager knowingly acted on their behalf. One admin can apply this standard using a verified admission or attributable evidence corroborated by the fixture's submission record. Circumstantial signals such as shared IP addresses or similar tactics are insufficient by themselves. If the evidence meets the standard, log the fixture, evidence reference and reason before extending the ban. Otherwise, close the report without action.

An owner can request a correction by identifying a specific error and supplying the missing context. One Ballerz admin can revoke the team and wallet penalties from that incident and log the correction. A different admin handles the request when available.

If the affected tie is still undecided when the ban is revoked, restore the team's place and give both sides a fair team-sheet deadline. If the forfeit is already official, restore future eligibility and annotate the forfeit with the revocation. The published bracket stands.

The first correction request filed before kickoff that identifies a specific error and provides an evidence reference holds the forfeit and the affected bracket slot. If the request is still open at kickoff, move the tie to a reserve date with a common new team-sheet deadline. The hold applies once for the same ban.

If the ban is upheld before the reserve deadline, resolve the tie under the normal forfeit rules. If the ban is revoked or the request remains open at that deadline, the match can go ahead with valid team sheets from both teams. Any later decision applies to future ties. A team that wins on the reserve date and later has its ban upheld forfeits its next tie, while the reserve-date result and any title already awarded stand.

The draw-based ownership restriction still runs until the cup edition closes, regardless of a team's results or exit.
